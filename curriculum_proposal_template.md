## 1. Executive Summary & Vision

This year, the curriculum will evolve from standalone Python and game development into an interconnected ecosystem. Students will not just write code for a screen; they will build **AI-driven gameplay mechanics** and study the bridge between the virtual and physical world using **microelectronics**.

### Core Pillars

* **Python Mastery:** Transitioning from syntax basics to object-oriented programming (OOP).
* **Game Development:** Utilizing Pygame or Godot to build scalable, interactive projects.
* **AI Integration:** Injecting machine learning models, pathfinding algorithms, and generative AI APIs into game workflows.
* **Microelectronics:** Using simulated or physical microcontrollers (e.g., Raspberry Pi Pico, Micro:bit) to create custom physical hardware controllers for their games.

## 2.1 Integrated Cohort Delivery & Peer Mentorship Model

To optimize our limited 2-hour Saturday morning sessions, the program will transition to a *unified digital environment* using the **Microsoft MakeCode Ecosystems, [MicroBit](https://makecode.microbit.org/) and [Arcade](https://arcade.makecode.com/) (Blocks & Python)**. This eliminates the split-attention burden on instruction and blends our cohorts seamlessly.

### ⏱️ Proposed Saturday Schedule (2-Hour Block)

To integrate the cohorts without dividing your attention, structure the two hours like a collaborative tech company:

* 10:00 AM – 10:20 AM | Unified Mini-Lesson (20 mins): You teach one universal core concept to everyone at once (e.g., Variables or Conditional Logic for Game AI).
* 10:20 AM – 11:00 AM | Guided Studio Application (40 mins): Scholars open MakeCode. You guide the flow, but students choose their input method. 3rd-5th graders build using blocks; 6th-8th graders implement the concept by toggling over to Python text.
* 11:00 AM – 11:40 AM | Peer Mentorship & Collaboration Block (40 mins): The Cohort Mixer. You pair older students with younger students.The Task: They work together on a shared digital challenge or game design project. The 6th–8th graders take on the role of "Senior Developers/Mentors," helping the 3rd–5th graders debug their code, brainstorm AI pathfinding logic, or design sprites.
* 11:40 AM – 12:00 PM | Showcase & Retro (20 mins): A few pairs share their simulator screens to show off what they built. Homework is assigned via Code.org for independent concept reinforcement during the week.

### Delivery Environment: Digital-First Micro:bit & Arcade Simulators

* **No-Friction Implementation:** To keep logistics lightweight and entirely digital, instruction will utilize MakeCode's robust built-in browser simulators.
* **Differentiated Coding Modes:** During independent work, 3rd–5th-grade scholars will build logic using MakeCode Blocks, while 6th–8th-grade scholars will utilize the exact same platform but write their code using the native Python text editor.
* **Asynchronous Homework:** Core programmatic concepts will be reinforced at home through targeted, self-paced modules on Code.org.

### The Peer Mentorship Co-Working Block

The middle portion of every session (40 minutes) will be designated as a collaborative studio block.

* **Middle School Mentors (Grades 6–8):** Act as "Lead Systems Engineers." They practice leadership and consolidate their own Python understanding by helping explain logic to younger peers.
* **Elementary Scholars (Grades 3–5):** Benefit from near-peer modeling, receiving immediate debugging support and technical encouragement.
* **Collaborative Goals:** Multi-age pairings will work together on creative digital design milestones, such as building a virtual simulation project or prototyping a game mechanic.

## 3. Deep Dive: New Domain Integration

### 🧠 Artificial Intelligence (AI) Implementation

* **Concepts taught:** Finite State Machines (FSMs), A* pathfinding, Neural Networks basics, Computer Vision (hand tracking for game controls).
* **Classroom Application:** Students will replace hard-coded NPC movements with smart pathfinding and build a game controlled entirely by hand gestures via webcam.

### 🔌 Microelectronics Integration

* **Concepts taught:** GPIO pins, analog vs. digital inputs, serial communication between hardware and Python.
* **Classroom Application:** Instead of playing games with a keyboard, students will wire up buttons, joysticks, and sensors on a breadboard to act as a custom physical game controller.
